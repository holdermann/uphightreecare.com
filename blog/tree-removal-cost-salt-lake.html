What Does Tree Removal Cost in Salt Lake City? An Honest Breakdown

August 19, 2026 · Up High Tree Care

Nobody loves paying to make a tree disappear, and the pricing can feel opaque: one neighbor paid a few hundred dollars, another paid several thousand, and both “had a tree removed.” Here’s what’s actually behind the difference.

The four things that set the price

Size. Height and trunk diameter drive the raw hours. A 25-foot ornamental plum is a morning; an 80-foot cottonwood is a full day for a full crew, plus serious hauling.

What’s underneath it. This is the factor people underestimate. A big tree over open lawn can be felled or dropped in large sections. The same tree over a roof, a fence line, a pool, or a power drop must be dismantled piece by piece, with every piece rigged and lowered on ropes. More rigging, more hours.

Access. Can a chip truck park near the work, or does every limb get carried through a 36-inch gate? Tight Avenues and Sugar House lots often add hours of hauling to otherwise simple jobs.

Condition. Dead and decayed trees are the dangerous ones — brittle wood doesn’t hold a climber or a rope the way live wood does, and the work slows down accordingly. It’s one of the best arguments for removing a dead tree sooner rather than later: the price generally goes up as the tree comes apart.

What’s included (and what to confirm)

A complete quote covers the removal, chipping and hauling the brush, and removing or bucking the trunk wood. Confirm three things in writing: whether wood hauling is included or the logs stay, whether the stump is included (grinding is separate equipment — bundling it is cheaper than a second visit), and that the company carries liability and workers’ comp insurance. That last one is the whole ballgame: an uninsured crew’s accident can become the homeowner’s problem.

Red flags worth knowing

Door-knockers “already working in the neighborhood,” quotes that drop steeply the moment you hesitate, pressure to decide today, and any suggestion of topping a tree instead of removing it. Good tree work has a paper trail: a written estimate, proof of insurance on request, and an invoice that matches the quote.

Get a real number

Ranges on the internet (including this one) can’t see your tree. A firm number takes a walk around the property — ours are free, written, and firm, anywhere in the Salt Lake Valley. If the honest answer is “this tree needs a trim, not a removal,” that’s the answer you’ll get.
