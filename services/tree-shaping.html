Tree Shaping & Training Services

Some trees are features, not just shade. Ornamental maples by the front walk, a row of flowering pears along the drive, globe willows, arborvitae screens — these earn their place by their form, and keeping that form takes a different kind of cutting than clearance trimming.

Shaping is also how damaged trees come back. A tree that was topped by a previous owner, or lost its leader in a storm, can usually be retrained over two or three seasons into a sound, natural-looking crown. It’s patient work — a little each year — and it’s far cheaper than removing and replacing a mature tree.

What you get

Ornamental and specimen work

Japanese maples, flowering pears and plums, globe willows — shaped to flatter the species’ natural habit, not sheared into gumdrops.

Restoration of topped trees

We select the strongest regrowth, remove the rest, and rebuild a real crown over a few seasons. Most topped trees can be saved.

Young-tree training

A new tree shaped correctly in its first five years needs dramatically less (and cheaper) work for the rest of its life.

How it works

  1. 1

    Design conversation

    What should this tree look like in five years? We shape toward a target, not just "smaller."

  2. 2

    Shaping cuts

    Selective heading and reduction cuts that direct growth, made at the right points for the species.

  3. 3

    Follow-up schedule

    Shaping is a program, not a one-off — we’ll tell you when to have us back and what to expect in between.

Tree Shaping questions, answered

Can a topped tree be fixed? +

Usually, yes — with patience. Topping triggers a burst of weak, fast-growing shoots at each cut. Restoration means selecting the best-attached of those shoots to become new leaders, removing the competition, and repeating over two or three seasons until the tree has a sound crown again. The result is never quite what the original would have been, but it’s a real tree instead of a hazard.

Do you shear hedges and arborvitae? +

Yes — formal hedges, arborvitae privacy screens, and junipers are shaping work too. The key with arborvitae is never cutting back past the green growth into bare wood, because most won’t regrow from it. We shape them tight without crossing that line.
